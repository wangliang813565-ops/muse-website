## Compare the three routes

### 1. Existing products

Choose this route when a collectible or useful gift already suits the recipients. The decision is about the exact item, presentation and delivery—not developing a new product.

**Confirm before ordering:** the selected variant, available quantity, packaging, delivery destination and written schedule. Catalogue visibility alone does not reserve stock.

[Browse the collection](/products/).

### 2. Existing products with branded presentation

Choose this route when you want a recognisable company gift without redesigning the object. Start with a card, sleeve, gift box or selected decoration method suited to the product.

**Confirm before production:** artwork dimensions, colours, language, print position and the approval sample. For existing characters, settle permitted branding and usage rights before finalising artwork.

[Explore merchandise customisation](/custom/).

### 3. An original character or custom object

Choose this route when the gift itself needs to express a mascot, campaign or brand story. Brief the character, format and packaging as one project, then agree how designs and samples will be approved.

**Confirm before production:** design ownership or licence, materials and dimensions, intended audience, sample acceptance, quantity and the delivery plan. For products intended for children, include the applicable age and product-safety requirements in the brief.

[Explore IP collaboration](/collaboration/) or [meet Hala](/hala/).

## Compare the complete proposal, not just the unit price

Ask for product, artwork or tooling, samples, packaging, freight and any applicable taxes or clearance charges to be itemised. A quoted unit price is useful only when the quantity, specification and delivery scope match.

Set out who approves artwork and the physical sample. Keep the agreed version, materials and packaging together so the production order matches the sample you accepted.

## Send a five-point brief

1. **Occasion:** a launch, employee programme, client gift or hospitality project.
2. **Recipients:** who will receive it and how they will use or display it.
3. **Quantity:** total units and any split between locations.
4. **Delivery:** required date and destination city.
5. **Branding:** packaging only, product decoration or an original design.
