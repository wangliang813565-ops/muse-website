## Maytree Revisits the Zodiac — This Time With a Spray Can

Maytree has already proven it knows how to handle the zodiac. The brand's [Zodiac blind box series](/blog/2026-05-18-maytree-zodiac-blind-boxes-dubai-collectors/) — two seasons of 13.5cm vinyl figures wrapped in celestial elegance and matte finishes — has been one of the strongest performers in the GCC designer toy market since its Dubai debut. That series was all restraint: subtle star-chart motifs, sculptural zodiac references that whispered rather than shouted, a colour palette that looked as comfortable in a curated living room as it did on a collector's shelf.

Zodic Doodle (SKU HX50920) takes the same twelve signs and runs them through an entirely different filter. If the original Zodiac series was an observatory at midnight, Zodic Doodle is a back-alley mural at two in the afternoon — loud, kinetic, and unapologetically urban. It is Maytree demonstrating that an IP does not need to repeat itself to stay coherent. The zodiac is the thread. The treatment is brand new.

## What Zodic Doodle Actually Is

Zodic Doodle is a twelve-design blind box keychain series. Each sign — Aries through Pisces — gets rendered as a compact keyring figure with a graffiti-inspired visual language: spray-paint splatters, marker-style outlines, hand-drawn star symbols, and bold colour blocking that reads as street art rather than illustration. The keychains are small enough to clip onto a bag or a set of keys but detailed enough to reward close inspection. The paint-splatter effects are not printed decals — they are sculpted into the surface, giving each figure a tactile dimension that flat printing cannot replicate.

The format matters. Keychains occupy a different collecting psychology than shelf figures. A shelf figure is destination decor — you place it and step back. A keychain travels with you. It gets handled, photographed at café tables, spotted across a room on someone else's bag. That portability changes how the design is experienced. The graffiti aesthetic, with its roots in public visibility and street-level presence, makes conceptual sense for a product designed to be seen in motion rather than on display.

The blind box mechanic remains, which means the pull — the moment of discovering which sign is inside — is part of the value. Twelve signs, any of which could be in the box. For astrology-oriented collectors, the emotional stakes are higher than a typical blind box: pulling your own sign carries a small charge of personal recognition that pulling a random character from a generic series does not.

## Why Graffiti Works for the Zodiac

On paper, graffiti and astrology seem like an odd pairing. One is raw, immediate, tied to urban space and countercultural energy. The other is ancient, systematic, rooted in celestial observation and archetypal symbolism. But the tension between them is precisely what makes Zodic Doodle interesting.

Astrology in the GCC is not a fringe interest. It has deep cultural roots — the region's astronomical traditions stretch back centuries — and it remains a mainstream conversation topic across Arabic and expat communities alike. But most zodiac merchandise treats the subject with a kind of hushed reverence: gold foil, midnight-blue backgrounds, serif typography. Zodic Doodle rejects that tone entirely. It treats the zodiac as something alive and contemporary rather than something to be preserved behind glass.

The graffiti approach also solves a design problem that plagues zodiac collectibles: how do you make twelve characters feel distinct without resorting to literal animal costumes? A Leo rendered as a lion-man in a cape is predictable. A Leo rendered through aggressive red-and-orange colour blocking, jagged marker strokes, and a crown motif that looks like it was tagged on a wall — that is a point of view. The graffiti language gives each sign a stylistic signature without leaning on obvious iconography.

## The Keychain as a Collecting Gateway

There is a practical argument for Zodic Doodle that has nothing to do with aesthetics. Keychain blind boxes are one of the lowest-barrier entry points into designer toy collecting. They cost less than full-scale figures. They take up no shelf space. They can be bought one at a time without the completion anxiety that comes with committing to a twelve-figure display series. For someone curious about designer toys but unsure where to start, a zodiac keychain that might match their star sign is an easier first purchase than a 13.5cm vinyl figure in a sealed box.

This does not mean Zodic Doodle is a compromise product. The design quality — sculpted paint effects, reinforced attachment hardware, consistent character work across all twelve signs — puts it firmly in the collector-grade category. But its format makes it accessible in a way that shelf figures are not. Maytree is building multiple on-ramps into its zodiac universe, and Zodic Doodle is the most approachable one yet.

For existing Maytree collectors, Zodic Doodle serves a different function. It extends the zodiac collection into a wearable, portable format without duplicating what the blind box figures already do. A collector who owns the full season-one shelf set can add the keychain versions of the same signs and feel like they are expanding the collection, not repeating it. The two formats coexist because they are experienced differently — one on a shelf, one on a bag.
